# Nollie privacy policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026.

Nollie is an app for running your own AI assistant, Hermes, on your Mac and using it from your
iPhone and iPad. It is made by Corey Kozarski, an independent developer in London.

## The short version
- Nollie has no servers and no accounts. Your chats stay between your devices and your own Mac.
- Nollie does not collect, sell or share your data, and has no analytics, ads or tracking.
- When you choose an online model (for example your ChatGPT or Claude plan), your messages go to
  that provider under your own account, and their privacy policy applies.

## What goes where
- **Your chats and files** go from the app to your own Hermes, which runs on your Mac.
- **Replies** come from the model you picked: one on your Mac, or an online provider you signed in to.
- **Web research** your bots do goes from your Mac to search services and websites over your own
  internet connection.
- **Notifications** when the app is closed: a short preview (up to 240 characters) is stored in
  your private iCloud database and deleted after 7 days. You can turn previews off, and bots you
  mark Private never send previews.
- **Sign-in details** for your Hermes stay in each device's Keychain.
- **On your Mac**, everything else Nollie writes lives in one folder,
  `~/Library/Application Support/Nollie`, apart from its settings, the permissions macOS keeps and its
  logs in `~/Library/Logs/Nollie`.
- **Logs** stay on your device. Private details such as chat text are left out of them. Settings ›
  About › Diagnostics makes a file you can share if you choose; nothing is sent on its own.
- **Updates** for the Mac app downloaded from the website: once a day Nollie asks GitHub, where its
  releases live, whether a newer version exists. GitHub sees that request like any web visit. You can
  turn this off in Settings › About.
- **Setup downloads** (the Hermes engine and an optional local model) come from their public
  release pages, checked before use.
- **Crash reports** reach the developer only through Apple, and only if you agreed to share
  analytics with developers on your device.

## Your choices
You can remove a bot, mark it Private, turn notification previews off or sign out of a model provider
at any time.

You can also remove Nollie from your Mac in Settings › About › Remove. It deletes what Nollie set up
there: its assistant with its chats and memory, its folder, settings, keychain items and background
helpers, Cua if Nollie installed it, and Nollie's add-ons in a Hermes of your own. If you ask, it
also deletes Nollie's alerts in your iCloud. Two things stay, because they aren't Nollie's to remove:
the permissions you gave Nollie in Privacy & Security, which you can remove there, and a Hermes you
set up yourself, with its own bots, chats and memory. On iPhone and iPad, the same place removes
Nollie's data on that device. [How removing Nollie works](../help/remove-nollie.md)

## Children
Nollie is not meant for children under 13 (or the minimum age where you live).
